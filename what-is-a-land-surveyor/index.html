What Is a Land Surveyor?

A land surveyor measures and interprets information about land, boundaries, and physical features. Property owners, buyers, builders, and design teams use that information to make decisions about a site. The type of survey you need depends on the question you are trying to answer.

What does a land surveyor do?

Surveying combines research with measurements of the property. Records such as deeds and plats help establish the documentary background, while fieldwork provides evidence of conditions on the ground. The resulting map, report, or staking work is prepared for a defined purpose.

A fence, an online parcel map, or a visual estimate does not answer every boundary question. Explain your planned use before ordering work so the survey scope can address the actual need.

Types of surveying and related services

ALTA/NSPS land title surveys

For commercial real estate transactions when the lender, title insurer, or other parties require an ALTA/NSPS survey. Discuss the title commitment and requested survey requirements early. Explore this service.

Boundary surveys

For questions about property lines, corners, and the relationship between recorded descriptions and conditions on the ground. Explore this service.

Topographic surveys

For site design that needs elevation, contours, and existing physical features. These measurements help designers understand the terrain. Explore this service.

Drone/UAV surveys

For suitable projects needing aerial imagery, LiDAR, photogrammetry, or mapping deliverables. The method depends on site conditions and the required accuracy. Explore this service.

Construction layout and staking

For transferring approved design positions to the site so construction teams can locate planned improvements. Explore this service.

Land consulting and due diligence

For evaluating property questions and coordinating information before a development or transaction. Consulting supports a project; it is not a substitute for every required survey. Explore this service.

Which survey should you request?

If you are buying commercial property, ask the lender and title company what they require. If you are planning a fence or addition, start by explaining the boundary or design question. If a design team needs elevations, discuss a topographic survey. Construction staking requires the appropriate project plans.

Some projects need more than one service. The Land Consultants can help clarify the scope after reviewing the site and the intended use of the work.

Information that helps define the project

  • Property address, parcel information, and approximate acreage.
  • Available deed, recorded plat, prior survey, or title commitment.
  • The reason for the survey and any lender, designer, or contractor requirements.
  • Your target date and known access restrictions.
