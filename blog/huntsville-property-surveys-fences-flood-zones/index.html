Property Surveys Start With the Purpose of Your Project

A property survey is not a one-size-fits-all service. The right type depends on the property, the purpose of the project, and the information needed to move forward.

A licensed land surveyor reviews relevant property records and takes field measurements to document important details about the site. Depending on the scope, this may include property boundaries and corners, existing improvements, easements, elevations, contours, utilities, access points, and other site conditions.

This information can support many property-related decisions, including real estate transactions, fence installation, new construction, site planning, subdivision, construction layout, due diligence, and flood-related elevation needs.

Property Line Surveys Before Fences and Improvements

One of the most common reasons to call a property surveyor is uncertainty about where a parcel begins and ends.

A fence may appear to follow a boundary, but its physical location alone does not establish the legal property line. The same applies to landscaping, driveways, tree lines, walls, and other visible features.

A boundary survey is used to establish the limits of a parcel based on recorded documents and field evidence.

Our boundary survey process may include:

  • Deed and property record research
  • Review of recorded plats and adjoining property information
  • Field investigation for monuments and other boundary evidence
  • Analysis of field measurements against available records
  • Preparation of the appropriate survey deliverable

This process helps property owners understand where property corners and boundary lines are before moving forward with improvements.

A property line survey can be especially useful when planning a fence, addition, or other improvement close to a boundary. Establishing the line before construction helps reduce the risk of relying on assumptions about where one property ends and another begins. Our guide to fence and property line surveys in Madison County covers that scenario in more detail.

Boundary surveys may also be needed when:

  • Buying or selling property
  • Dividing a parcel of land
  • Evaluating possible encroachments
  • Clarifying uncertainty between adjoining properties

Property corners are just as important as the lines connecting them. Surveyors research property records and look for existing monuments and other physical evidence in the field. Measurements are then evaluated alongside deeds, plats, and adjoining property information.

For this reason, an old stake, fence corner, or marker found on a property should not automatically be treated as the legal property corner without professional evaluation.

A found marker is evidence, not a conclusion

An iron pin in the ground tells a surveyor that someone once set something there. What it does not tell you is who set it, for which parcel, or whether it agrees with the deed. That evaluation — record against monument, monument against measurement — is the survey.

The goal of a boundary survey is not simply to produce measurements. It is to evaluate the available record and field evidence so the property’s boundaries can be professionally determined and documented.

Topographic Surveys Turn the Ground Into Useful Data

Boundaries show where a parcel begins and ends. Topography answers another important question: What is happening across the surface of the site?

A topographic survey measures elevations and maps the physical features of a property. The resulting information may include contours and existing site features that architects, engineers, developers, and other project professionals need for planning and design.

For Huntsville-area properties with changing terrain, detailed elevation information can be especially useful when evaluating how a site may be developed.

Changes in elevation can influence several parts of a project, including:

  • Grading and drainage planning
  • Road and driveway design
  • Building placement
  • Utility and infrastructure planning
  • Overall site-development decisions

Our land surveying services include topographic surveys using technologies such as LiDAR and photogrammetry alongside professional surveying methods.

A topographic survey may be needed when:

  • Designing a new building or site improvement
  • Developing grading or drainage plans
  • Planning roads, utilities, or other infrastructure
  • Evaluating an undeveloped parcel
  • Preparing engineering or architectural designs
  • Documenting existing site conditions

Collecting accurate information about existing site conditions early gives the professionals designing the project a stronger foundation for planning and making informed decisions.

Flood Zones Require More Than a Visual Guess

Flood-related questions are another reason property owners may need professional surveying services. A property may appear relatively flat or far from visible water while still requiring closer evaluation of mapped flood hazards or building elevations.

FEMA provides flood hazard information used to identify mapped flood risk areas. When specific elevation documentation for a structure is required, an Elevation Certificate may be appropriate. It documents a building’s elevation in relation to applicable flood information.

The type of survey needed depends on the purpose. A boundary survey focuses on property boundaries, while a topographic survey documents site elevations and physical features. An Elevation Certificate serves a specific flood-related documentation purpose.

Commercial Properties Often Need ALTA/NSPS Surveys

Residential boundary questions are only one part of professional surveying. Commercial real estate transactions often require more detailed information about title matters, access, easements, improvements, and other conditions affecting a property.

An ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey follows nationally recognized standards established for these types of transactions. Our surveying services include ALTA/NSPS surveys for commercial real estate needs in Huntsville and surrounding areas.

An ALTA/NSPS survey can coordinate survey information with title documents and specific transaction requirements. Depending on the required scope and applicable Table A items, it may address easements, rights-of-way, improvements, access, utilities, and other relevant property conditions.

This level of detail can be valuable for commercial buyers, lenders, title professionals, attorneys, developers, and investors conducting due diligence. Having a clearer understanding of the property can help these parties evaluate important conditions before moving forward with a transaction or development project.

The survey scope should be coordinated early in the process. Providing the surveyor with available title materials and transaction requirements helps establish what needs to be shown and which optional items have been requested. Our article on the 2026 ALTA/NSPS standards walks through what changed and how to order this survey type correctly the first time. For commercial properties in Huntsville, this can be particularly useful before acquisition, financing, redevelopment, or other significant property decisions.

Drone and LiDAR Technology Expands Surveying Capabilities

Modern survey companies may use different technologies depending on the site, project scope, and required deliverables. At The Land Consultants, our capabilities include drone/UAV LiDAR and photogrammetry as part of our professional surveying work.

These tools can be especially useful for large sites, difficult terrain, detailed data collection, or areas that may be challenging to cover efficiently through ground observations alone.

Our drone/UAV surveying services use both LiDAR and photogrammetry. LiDAR uses laser measurements to collect detailed spatial data and can capture ground information through gaps in vegetation. Photogrammetry uses overlapping aerial images to create mapped imagery and three-dimensional information.

Depending on the needs of the project, drone surveying can support:

  • Topographic mapping
  • Large development-site data collection
  • Construction progress documentation
  • Corridor mapping
  • Volumetric calculations
  • Orthomosaic imagery
  • Three-dimensional terrain and point-cloud data

Drone technology does not replace professional surveying. Its value comes from combining aerial data with appropriate survey control, professional surveying methods, and the specific requirements of each project.

Construction Layout Takes Design From Plans to the Site

Surveying can continue after planning and design. Construction layout and staking turn design plans into physical points in the field, helping contractors locate buildings, foundations, roads, utilities, grades, and other improvements.

A project may require several surveying services as it progresses. Boundary work can establish property limits, topographic surveys can support design, and construction staking can guide planned improvements. Involving a land surveyor early can help determine what information is needed at each stage.
