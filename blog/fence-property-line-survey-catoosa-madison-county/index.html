Building a Fence in Catoosa County or Madison County: Why You Need a Property Line Survey First

A survey before you dig prevents encroachment disputes, wasted materials, and forced fence removal.

All Articles
Fences Property Lines Catoosa County, GA Madison County, AL
February 9, 2026 6 min read The Land Consultants

Erecting a residential privacy fence without confirming property boundaries often leads to severe legal and financial consequences. Property owners who proceed without a professional land survey risk constructing a fence past their legal boundary line. This minor error constitutes an encroachment. In these scenarios, neighbors can legally compel the property owner to dismantle the new structure at their own expense, resulting in complete loss of construction materials, wasted labor, and damaged neighborly relations.4

To avoid these disputes, property owners should hire a licensed land surveyor to physically locate and mark the legal boundaries of the parcel.10 Licensed surveyors conduct historical deed research, locate buried iron monumentation using specialized magnetic locators, and place visible stakes along the property line.3 This process ensures the fence builder can install the structure with complete legal confidence.4

Catoosa County, Georgia: Local Fence Rules

A comparative analysis of the regulatory frameworks in Catoosa County, Georgia, and Madison County, Alabama, demonstrates why a property line survey is crucial prior to construction. In the unincorporated areas of Catoosa County, Georgia, building permits are not required to erect, repair, or dismantle a standard residential fence, unless the structure encroaches on a public right-of-way or encloses a swimming pool.12 While Catoosa County zoning ordinances do not mandate a specific setback distance from property lines, the fence must reside entirely within the owner’s deeded boundary.12 Violations of these zoning codes are prosecuted in the Catoosa County Magistrate Court, which can impose fines of up to $1,000 per day per violation and sentences of up to 60 days in jail.13 Furthermore, Georgia state law requires a mandatory call to Georgia 811 at least two to eight business days before any excavation begins to prevent damaging underground utilities.12

Madison County & Huntsville, Alabama: Local Fence Rules

In Madison County, Alabama, and the city of Huntsville, fence regulations are also highly localized. Huntsville does not require a building permit for standard residential fences, but homeowners must adhere to strict zoning parameters.14 Front yard fences are capped at 4 feet, while side and rear yard fences are limited to 6 feet.14 On corner lots, a clear-view zone restricts fences near intersections, prohibiting any obstruction between 2.5 and 15 feet in height within the sight triangle.14 Furthermore, if a property is located within one of Huntsville’s designated historic districts, the homeowner must obtain a certificate of appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Commission before any work begins.14

Alabama’s Partition Fence Law (Title 35, Chapter 7) also establishes that coterminous landowners of improved properties share proportional responsibility for the costs of building and maintaining shared boundary fences.14 If a neighbor utilizes an existing fence as part of their enclosure, they are legally obligated to pay their proportionate share of its value.14 If the neighbors cannot reach an agreement, either party can petition the district court to appoint “fence-viewers” to assess the fence and issue an enforceable cost determination.14 To legally enforce this shared liability, a precise property line survey is required to prove that the fence sits directly on the shared boundary line.14

Survey first, build once

Whether your project falls under Georgia or Alabama rules, the common requirement is the same: the fence must sit within your deeded boundary. A property line survey with staked corners is the only way to build with legal confidence and to enforce shared-cost fence laws.
