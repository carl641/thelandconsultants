Do I Need a Land Survey Before Buying a House in North Alabama or Northwest Georgia?

A closing survey, title insurance, and GIS tax maps each leave a dangerous gap. Here is what actually protects a buyer.

All Articles
Home Buyers Due Diligence North Alabama Northwest Georgia
May 8, 2026 7 min read The Land Consultants

Investing in residential real estate in high-growth markets like Huntsville, Alabama, or Ringgold, Georgia, is a major financial commitment. To protect this investment, buyers should secure a professional boundary survey during the due diligence phase of the transaction.4 Many buyers mistakenly believe that a standard lender’s “closing survey” or a title insurance policy provides sufficient protection against boundary issues, which leaves them vulnerable to significant financial risk.3

Why a Closing Survey and Title Insurance Fall Short

A mortgage closing survey is a basic lot inspection designed solely to confirm that a home sits within its general subdivision boundaries.3 This limited inspection does not include the physical recovery or placement of corner stakes, nor does it identify minor physical encroachments along the boundaries.3 Similarly, standard title insurance policies contain a standard survey exception. This clause explicitly excludes coverage for any boundary overlaps, area shortages, easements, or encroachments that a current, accurate land survey would have revealed. If a boundary dispute arises after the closing, the buyer must resolve the issue without assistance from their title insurance provider.

The GIS Tax Map Trap

Relying on county GIS tax maps to estimate property boundaries is also a common and risky mistake.17 These maps are created by county assessors solely for property tax valuations, not to establish legal boundaries.17 Tax maps are frequently based on un-orthorectified aerial photography, which can lead to spatial offsets of several feet.17 Consequently, using a GIS map to plan improvements like fences or driveways often leads to encroachment disputes with neighbors.

What a Full Boundary Survey Actually Delivers

A comprehensive boundary survey conducted by a licensed land surveyor resolves these risks through several key steps:

  • Deed Tracing and Verification: The surveyor traces the legal description back through historical land records to resolve omissions, gaps, or overlaps in the chain of title.3
  • Physical Encroachment Identification: The survey identifies physical encroachments, such as a neighbor’s driveway crossing the property line, retaining walls built over the boundary, or structural roof overhangs.4
  • Easement Mapping: The surveyor maps recorded utility, drainage, and municipal easements, showing where future construction is restricted.4
  • Physical Corner Staking: Field crews place permanent iron pins or markers at every corner of the property, providing clear, visible references for future improvements.3

Investing in a professional land survey prior to closing provides buyers with clear, verified information about the land they are purchasing. This proactive measure prevents costly legal disputes and protects the buyer’s long-term real estate investment.4

Before you sign

A full boundary survey is the only one of these three options that physically marks your corners, detects encroachments, and produces a legally defensible plat. For a major purchase, it is inexpensive insurance against years of dispute.
